Oktoberfest UK 2026: Where to Celebrate and What to Expect

Where to celebrate Oktoberfest in the UK in 2026 — city-by-city events, what to expect, booking tips, and a year-round alternative for beer lovers.

Every autumn, Britain gets a little bit Bavarian. Steins are hoisted, oompah bands strike up, lederhosen emerge from the back of wardrobes, and Oktoberfest events spring up in cities from Glasgow to Cardiff. You no longer need a flight to Munich to join in — though the UK versions do things their own way, and knowing what to expect makes for a much better night. Here is our guide to Oktoberfest in the UK in 2026, plus a suggestion for anyone who wants the good bits of the festival all year round: a guided brewery tour with people who take beer just as seriously, minus the queue for the toilets.

First, the original

The real Oktoberfest happens in Munich and, despite the name, mostly in September — it traditionally opens in the second half of the month and runs into the first days of October. It began in 1810 as a royal wedding celebration and grew into the world's biggest folk festival, with vast beer tents run by Munich's historic breweries. If 2026 is your year for the pilgrimage, check the official Oktoberfest website for confirmed dates and practical advice, and book accommodation far earlier than feels reasonable.

Oktoberfest events across the UK in 2026

Britain's take on Oktoberfest has grown enormously, and most big cities now get at least one large event each autumn. In London, expect multiple large-scale tented festivals plus the capital's German-style beer halls running special programmes through the season — London even brews its own Munich-style lagers these days, with breweries like German Kraft making fresh festbier-style beers on home soil. Manchester has one of the strongest claims outside the capital: alongside touring tent events, Albert's Schloss — the city's much-loved Bavarian bier palace — treats the season as its natural home turf. If you are heading that way, our Manchester guide covers the city's wider beer scene.

Elsewhere, touring Oktoberfest events return to cities including Birmingham, Leeds, Liverpool, Newcastle, Glasgow, Edinburgh, Bristol and Cardiff most years, typically landing on autumn weekends in big-top tents or exhibition halls. One golden rule applies to all of them: dates, venues and ticket prices change from year to year and events do sell out, so always check the official sites before booking — and be wary of resold tickets.

What to actually expect

A UK Oktoberfest is a party first and a beer event second, and it is best enjoyed in that spirit. Expect long communal benches, live oompah bands that gradually give way to pop singalongs, bratwurst and pretzels, and beer served by the stein — usually German imports or German-style lagers. Fancy dress is optional but widespread; by mid-evening you will feel underdressed without at least a checked shirt.

For the beer-curious, there is a nice detail worth knowing: the beer served at Munich's festival today is festbier, a strong golden lager, while the style historically associated with the festival is märzen — a richer, amber, toastier lager brewed in March and matured over summer. Plenty of British brewers release märzens and festbiers in autumn, so keep an eye on taproom boards through September and October even if the big tents are not your scene.

The food deserves a mention too. The classic tent menu — bratwurst, schnitzel, giant pretzels, roast chicken — is built to keep pace with strong lager, and eating well is the difference between lasting the session and leaving it early. Most UK events also cater reasonably for vegetarians these days, and alcohol-free German lagers are increasingly easy to find, so designated drivers are no longer stuck with cola.

Tips for a better Oktoberfest night

Book a table if the event offers them — standing room at a busy session is chaos, cheerfully meant. Go to an afternoon or weekday session for a more relaxed atmosphere and easier tickets; Saturday nights are the biggest and booziest. Eat properly, early: stein measures are deceptively large. Sort your journey home in advance, since thousands of people leave at the same time. And if you are organising a work do or a big birthday around it, have a fallback — popular sessions sell out weeks ahead.

Oktoberfest energy, all year round

Here is the thing about Oktoberfest: what people actually love is drinking good beer with friends in a place with real atmosphere, ideally with someone on hand who knows what they are pouring. That does not need to be limited to two weeks in autumn. We have been running them across the UK since 2014, in London, Leeds, Manchester, Liverpool, Bristol, Cardiff and beyond; you will find the best UK beer cities all covered.

And if you are wrangling a group — the same crowd you would take to Oktoberfest, say — our private group tours run in most UK cities and work brilliantly for birthdays, stags, hens and team days, whatever the season.

The bottom line

Oktoberfest in the UK in 2026 will be bigger than ever, and with a bit of planning it is a terrific night: check official sites for confirmed dates, book early, eat a pretzel, sing along. Then, when the tents are packed away and the lederhosen go back in the wardrobe, remember that Britain's breweries are open all year — and the beer is at its best right at the source.
