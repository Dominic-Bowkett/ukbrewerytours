Calories in Corona: The Bottle, the Pint and the Lime Question

How many calories are in Corona? Bottle and pint figures for Corona Extra and Cero, whether the lime wedge counts, and how it compares to UK lagers.

A 330ml bottle of Corona Extra contains about 140 calories. Poured as a full 568ml pint — increasingly common now that Corona is on draught in UK pubs — it comes to roughly 240. And the lime wedge everyone asks about? Call it one extra calorie. Squeeze away.

Corona has a reputation as a light, easy, beach-adjacent beer, so those numbers surprise people: calorie for calorie, it's up there with the heavier premium lagers on the British bar. Here's why, plus the figures for alcohol-free Corona Cero and how the whole family compares with the lagers it competes against.

Corona Extra: the numbers

Corona's published nutrition works out at around 42 calories per 100ml. Across the servings you'll actually encounter in the UK:

  • 330ml bottle: about 140 calories
  • Half pint (284ml): about 120 calories
  • Pint (568ml): about 240 calories

The usual caveat applies with feeling: these are approximate figures derived from widely published per-100ml numbers, and both the recipe and the UK strength have shifted over the years — Corona Extra currently sits at 4.5% ABV in Britain. Check the label for the bottle in your hand.

Why so many calories in something so pale and light-bodied? Because colour and body have almost nothing to do with it. Beer calories track alcohol above all else — about 7 calories per gram — and at 4.5%, with a decent slug of residual corn and malt sweetness behind it, Corona simply carries more energy than its sunshine branding suggests. Light in flavour is not light in calories; the two get confused constantly, and Corona might be the single best example of the mix-up.

The lime question, answered properly

A standard wedge of lime — about an eighth of the fruit — contains roughly one calorie. Even if you dropped in a whole lime's worth of wedges you'd add under ten. As a proportion of a 140-calorie bottle, the garnish is a rounding error. If you're weighing up whether to skip the lime for your waistline, don't bother: it's the one part of a Corona that's genuinely free.

Where the lime does earn scrutiny is the sugary imitations. Pre-mixed "beer with lime" products and lime-flavoured lagers usually get their citrus from syrup, and that can add 30 to 50 real calories a bottle. Fresh fruit, no. Flavoured versions, read the label.

Corona Cero: the light one that actually is light

Corona Cero, the 0.0% version now stocked pretty much everywhere, delivers what the parent beer's image promises. A 330ml bottle carries about 55–60 calories — well under half the full-strength figure — and it keeps the crisp, faintly sweet character, lime compatibility included. With the alcohol gone, the remaining calories are almost all carbohydrate, which is why no alcohol-free beer ever reaches zero.

Cero has become one of the go-to bottles for anyone pacing a session, and it holds its own in our ranked guide to the lowest calorie beers in the UK alongside Lucky Saint, Heineken 0.0 and Guinness 0.0.

Corona vs the UK lagers

Here's where Corona Extra lands against the beers it shares a fridge with, per 568ml pint equivalent, all approximate:

BeerABVApprox. kcal per pint
Foster's3.7%170
Carling4.0%190
Guinness Draught4.2%210
Birra Moretti4.6%215
Stella Artois4.6%227
Corona Extra4.5%240
San Miguel5.0%255

Notice something odd: Corona out-calories Stella Artois despite being fractionally weaker. That's the corn showing — Corona is brewed with a proportion of maize and finishes slightly less dry than the European lagers, so a little more unfermented carbohydrate survives into the glass. It's one of the few cases where two beers of near-identical strength are separated by recipe rather than alcohol. Even so, the gap is small; strength still explains most of the table, as our full calories in a pint of lager comparison shows across every major brand. And for the record, a pint of Peroni and a pint of Corona are near enough identical — pick on taste, not calories.

Keeping the beach beer beach-body friendly

The pleasant irony of Corona is that its natural serving size is the solution. The 330ml bottle is the classic serve, and at about 140 calories it's one of the more moderate orders at any bar — less than a large glass of wine, about level with a gin and tonic. The calorie trouble only starts when Corona moves to pint glasses, which turns a 140-calorie decision into a 240-calorie one.

So: stick to bottles, and the maths mostly takes care of itself. Alternate with a Cero if it's a long afternoon — that averages your bottles out at under 100 calories each. And keep half an eye on the units: at 4.5%, a bottle is 1.5 units, so the NHS's 14-a-week guideline is nine bottles, not the crate the sunshine suggests.

All figures approximate, as ever. Recipes change, strengths get adjusted between markets, and the label is the only source that's always up to date.
