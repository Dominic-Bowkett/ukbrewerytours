Calories in a Pint of Cider: From Bone Dry to Dark Fruit

How many calories are in a pint of cider? Thatchers, Strongbow, Aspall and the fruit ciders compared — and why dry cider beats sweet every time.

A pint of cider contains roughly 200 to 310 calories — a wider spread than you'll find in lager, and the reason is sugar. Beer's calories are dictated almost entirely by alcohol; cider adds a second variable, because some ciders are fermented bone dry while others have fruit juice and sweetness added back by the tankful. Two pints at the same strength can be 80 calories apart purely on sweetness.

Here's how the big UK cider brands compare, why dry and sweet are so different, and how cider stacks up against beer for anyone keeping count.

Calories in a pint of cider: the big brands compared

As with our pint of lager comparison, the figures below are worked out from widely published per-100ml numbers multiplied up to a 568ml pint and rounded. They're approximate — recipes and strengths change, so always check the label for the current figure.

Two patterns are worth noticing. First, Aspall earns its place near the top honestly — it's simply strong, at 5.5%. Second, the fruit ciders at the bottom of the table get there despite being weak. Old Mout and Rekorderlig are only 4%, yet they carry more calories per pint than a 5.5% traditional cider, because they're loaded with fruit juice and sugar. Strongbow Dark Fruit pulls the same trick at 4%: it manages to out-calorie standard Strongbow by about 55 calories a pint while containing less alcohol.

One practical note: most fruit ciders are sold in 500ml bottles rather than pints, which softens the numbers a little. A 500ml bottle of Old Mout is around 250 calories; a 500ml Rekorderlig around 270. Still chunky, but not quite the full pint figure.

Dry vs sweet: where the difference comes from

All cider starts the same way — apple juice, which is full of natural sugar, gets fermented by yeast. If the fermentation is allowed to finish, the yeast eats virtually all of that sugar and converts it to alcohol, leaving a dry cider with perhaps a gram or two of sugar per 100ml. Traditional dry and vintage ciders work this way, and their calories come mostly from the alcohol itself.

Sweet and fruit ciders take that dry base and add sweetness back — apple juice, fruit juice, sugar or sweeteners — before packaging. Every gram of added sugar is 4 extra calories, and it stacks on top of the alcohol calories rather than replacing them. That's how a modest 4% fruit cider ends up outweighing a strong traditional one.

The rule of thumb is simple: the drier the cider, the fewer the calories at any given strength. If you love cider and you're watching the numbers, moving from sweet to dry is worth more than moving from strong to weak. Some brands now publish "light" versions too — Strongbow Ultra and various slimline fruit ciders — which lean on sweeteners instead of sugar and can drop under 150 calories a pint. Approximate figures, as ever: check the label.

Cider vs beer: which is lighter?

Pint for pint, standard cider and standard beer are closer than most people expect. A pint of Strongbow at around 205 calories sits almost exactly level with a pint of Carling at around 190, and just below a pint of Guinness at about 210. At the everyday end of the market there's honestly not much in it.

The difference appears at the edges. Beer's range in a typical pub runs from about 170 to 260 calories a pint; cider's runs from about 200 to 310, and premium bottled ciders can go higher still. The sweet end of cider simply reaches heights that beer doesn't, because brewers can't add sugar to a lager without turning it into something else, while cider makers add it as a matter of course.

There's also the carbohydrate angle. Dry cider is actually one of the lower-carb drinks in the pub — most of its sugar is long gone — while fruit cider is one of the highest, carrying real sugar rather than the unfermentable traces found in beer. We've unpacked that properly in our guide to carbs in beer.

Keeping the cider, trimming the calories

  • Go dry. Traditional dry or "original" styles over dark fruit and tropical flavours. Biggest single saving available.
  • Watch the strength. A 4.5% cider beats a 5.5% one by around 50 calories a pint, all else being equal.
  • Halves and bottles. A half of Thatchers is about 125 calories; a 500ml bottle shaves an eighth off any pint figure.
  • Try the light versions. Sweetener-based ciders aren't to everyone's taste, but they solve the sugar problem at a stroke.
  • Alternate with something lighter. A round of alcohol-free beer mid-session barely registers — our ranking of the lowest calorie beers in the UK has plenty of candidates.

And the usual caveat, because it matters: every figure in this article is approximate. Cider makers reformulate more often than brewers, sugar contents shift between batches and bottle sizes vary. The label on the bottle is always the final word.
