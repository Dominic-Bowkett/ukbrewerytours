Liverpool Craft Beer: From the Baltic Triangle to the Georgian Quarter

A guide to Liverpool craft beer — Baltic Triangle breweries, historic pubs, the Cains and Higsons story, and the city's guided beer experience.

Liverpool does everything with personality, and its beer is no exception. Over the past decade the city has turned old warehouses into taprooms, revived the names of its lost breweries and built a craft beer scene with a warmth you can feel from the first pint. Better still, it is all wonderfully walkable — you can get from ornate Victorian pub to railway-arch brewery in minutes, with a laugh guaranteed at both ends. Our Liverpool guide covers the guided side of things; here is the bigger picture for anyone planning a beer-led visit.

The Baltic Triangle: Liverpool's craft beer heart

South of the city centre, the Baltic Triangle is where Liverpool's modern beer story is being written. This wedge of former industrial streets between the centre and the docks has become the city's creative quarter, and breweries were among the first through the door. Black Lodge Brewing is the neighbourhood's home-grown independent, brewing on site with a taproom that captures the area's DIY spirit. Nearby, Love Lane Brewing set up in a huge converted warehouse and made headlines by reviving Higsons — the beloved Liverpool brewery name that generations of Scousers grew up with. Weekend afternoons are the liveliest time to visit; opening hours vary through the week, so check each brewery's site before you set out.

What makes the Baltic special is not any single taproom but the density of the whole thing. Within a few streets you can move between breweries, street food, record shops and music venues, and the crowd mixes craft beer devotees with people who have simply wandered in and stayed. Beers from across Merseyside and the wider North West appear on guest lines everywhere, so even a short session gives you a decent survey of the region's brewing.

Cains Brewery Village: a brewery reborn

At the southern edge of the Baltic sits one of Liverpool's most striking beer landmarks: the grand red-brick Cains brewery. Cains was the city's other great brewing name, and though large-scale brewing ended there, the Victorian site has found a new life as Cains Brewery Village — a sprawling complex of bars, food halls and independent traders where you can drink in the shadow of the old brewhouse. It is touristy in the best way: big, sociable and very Liverpool. Between Higsons and Cains, the city's brewing heritage is not so much remembered as actively lived in.

The Georgian Quarter and the city's great pubs

Liverpool's historic pubs are worth a trip on their own. The Philharmonic Dining Rooms — "the Phil" to everyone local — is one of the most ornate pubs in Britain, a riot of mosaic, mahogany and marble that famously extends even to the gents' toilets. Up the hill in the Georgian Quarter you will find creaky, characterful locals like Ye Cracke, with its Beatles connections, while back towards the business district the Ship & Mitre has been a beer destination for decades, known for one of the widest and best-kept beer ranges in the city. Add specialist craft beer bars like Dead Crafty in the city centre and you have a crawl that covers three centuries of Liverpool drinking without ever getting boring.

The guided option: Liverpool Craft Beer Experience

If you want the inside track, our Liverpool Craft Beer Experience is the easiest way to get it. Running on the last Saturday of each month, it is a four-hour guided walk through the city's independent beer scene: four breweries and taprooms, ten craft beer tastings, and a flat, easy city-centre route with no strenuous walking. Fittingly, it starts inside the Ship & Mitre. Your guide is a local beer specialist who takes you behind the scenes at working breweries, talks you through every style in your glass and shares the stories behind the city's brewing revival — from the Higsons years to the Baltic's new wave. It is sociable, unhurried and suits everyone from committed beer geeks to friends who just fancy a brilliant Saturday. Booking ahead is sensible, as monthly dates fill quickly.

A perfect beer day in Liverpool

Here is how we would spend it. Start with a late-morning wander through the Georgian Quarter and a look inside the Phil — go early, before the crowds. Head down to the Baltic Triangle for the afternoon: a taproom session at one or two breweries, then an hour amongst the traders at Cains Brewery Village. As evening arrives, work back into the centre via the Ship & Mitre for something rare from the fridges, and finish wherever the city takes you — Liverpool has never needed help providing a good night out. If your weekend stretches further, the city holds its own against any of the UK's best beer cities, and it is arguably the friendliest of the lot.

Practical notes

Liverpool Lime Street puts you fifteen minutes' walk from almost everything in this guide, and the flat centre makes a car unnecessary — helpful, given the itinerary. Taproom culture peaks Friday to Sunday, and some Baltic venues keep limited midweek hours, so check sites before travelling. Football weekends bring huge, happy crowds; book tables and tours early if your visit coincides with a match.
