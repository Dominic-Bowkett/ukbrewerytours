A 330ml bottle of Peroni Nastro Azzurro contains about 140 calories, and a 568ml pint poured on draught comes to roughly 240. That puts Peroni towards the upper end of the UK lager market — not because there's anything indulgent hiding in the recipe, but for the simplest reason in brewing: at 5.1% ABV, it's stronger than almost everything it shares a bar with.

Here's the full picture — bottle, pint, half and the alcohol-free Libera 0.0 — plus how Peroni compares with the other lagers on the pump.

## The quick numbers

Peroni's published figure works out at around 42 calories per 100ml. Multiply that across the usual serving sizes and you get:

- **330ml bottle:** about 140 calories
- **Half pint (284ml):** about 120 calories
- **Pint (568ml):** about 240 calories

As always with beer, treat these as approximate. They're derived from the brewer's widely published per-100ml figures, and recipes and strengths do change over time — the label or the official site has the current numbers.

## Why Peroni sits at the top of the table

There's a persistent idea that Peroni, being pale, crisp and Italian, must somehow be a lighter choice. The style is light; the calories aren't. Alcohol is the densest thing in any beer at roughly 7 calories per gram, and Peroni carries more of it than most mainstream lagers. A 5.1% beer starts every comparison 60-odd calories per pint behind a 4% one, and no amount of Italian styling can undo the arithmetic.

Here's where Peroni lands against the beers it competes with, per 568ml pint, all approximate:

| Beer | ABV | Approx. kcal per pint |
|---|---|---|
| Foster's | 3.7% | 170 |
| Carling | 4.0% | 190 |
| Guinness Draught | 4.2% | 210 |
| Birra Moretti | 4.6% | 215 |
| Stella Artois | 4.6% | 227 |
| **Peroni Nastro Azzurro** | **5.1%** | **240** |
| San Miguel | 5.0% | 255 |

Two comparisons stand out. Fellow Italian Birra Moretti undercuts Peroni by about 25 calories a pint, almost entirely because it's brewed at 4.6% rather than 5.1% — if you want the Italian lager experience at a discount, that's the swap. And [a pint of Guinness](/blog/calories-in-pint-of-guinness/), the beer everyone assumes is the heavy one, comes in about 30 calories lighter than a pint of Peroni. Pale and crisp is a flavour, not a calorie count.

The full brand-by-brand rundown, including Madrí, Carlsberg and the rest, is in our guide to the [calories in a pint of lager](/blog/calories-in-a-pint-of-lager/).

## Peroni Libera 0.0: the escape hatch

If you love the Peroni ritual but not the numbers, Libera 0.0 is the brand's alcohol-free version, and it changes the sums completely. A 330ml bottle carries roughly 70–80 calories — about half the full-strength bottle — while keeping the dry, faintly grassy character that makes Nastro Azzurro what it is. It's one of the more convincing alcohol-free lagers from a big brewer, and because the alcohol is gone, the remaining calories come almost entirely from carbohydrate.

That halving rather than vanishing is worth understanding: alcohol-free beers still contain malt sugars, so they're light but not weightless. ## What about Peroni Gluten Free and the red label?

A couple of variants worth a line each. Peroni Nastro Azzurro Gluten Free is brewed to the same 5.1% strength and lands within a few calories of the standard bottle — going gluten-free changes what the beer is made from, not how much alcohol it carries. Peroni Red, the original 4.7% version occasionally spotted in the UK, sits slightly lighter, at roughly 220 calories per pint equivalent. Same rule as ever: strength drives the figure.

## Is Peroni "fattening"?

No more than any other beer of its strength — which is to say, it depends entirely on how many you have. One bottle of Nastro Azzurro is a modest 140 calories, less than a large glass of wine and far less than most cocktails. Where it stings is the pint-after-pint pattern: three pints of Peroni is around 720 calories, roughly a quarter of a typical adult's day, and the 5.1% strength means the units stack up just as quickly — a pint is 2.9 units, so five pints busts the NHS weekly guideline of 14 in a single sitting.

The practical tricks are the obvious ones. Peroni suits the bottle serve better than most beers anyway — it's designed to be drunk cold, quickly, and looking vaguely glamorous — so letting the 330ml bottle do the portion control for you is no hardship. Alternating with a Libera 0.0 halves both the calories and the units without breaking the aesthetic. And if you're several deep, swapping the last round to a half is 120 calories you'll never miss.

Every figure here is approximate. Brewers adjust recipes, ABVs get trimmed, and per-100ml numbers move — check the label for the version in your hand.
